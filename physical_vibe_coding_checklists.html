Physical vibe coding: build checklists

Handout for the workshop on vibe coding robots that run in the real world. Draft.

  1. AI output is a hypothesis. The multimeter is the experiment.
  2. Every gate below is a measurement or a physical test, not a code review.
  3. Safe by default: if anything stops talking, the motors stop.

Audit of an AI-generated tutorial

The reference tutorial (4WD ESP8266 car, dual TB6612, web control) looks polished and gets a lot right. That is exactly why it is a good exercise: the mistakes are quiet ones. Use it as a “find the bug before you build” task.

SeverityWhat the tutorial doesWhy it fails in the physical worldFix
High Uses GPIO14 (D5) for both AIN2_LEFT and the ultrasonic ECHO, and says “that’s fine (output vs input)”. The last pinMode call wins, so the pin ends up an input and the left driver’s AIN2 is never driven. The divider’s 2 kΩ to ground also loads the line. The left side can’t reverse or turn properly. One job per pin. Free a pin (for example, share one GPIO for both STBY lines) or move to an ESP32, which has more usable GPIOs.
High MAX_SPEED = 180 passed to analogWrite, a value that suits 8-bit Arduino boards. On the ESP8266 core the default PWM range is 0 to 1023, so 180 is about 18% duty. The car is sluggish or won’t start, and the cause looks like “bad motors”. Call analogWriteRange(255) or scale to the real range. Confirm by measuring the average motor voltage.
High The web app sends “forward” on press and “stop” on release as two separate requests. The firmware has no timeout. A lost “stop” (Wi-Fi hiccup, phone locks, finger slides off the button) leaves the car driving. Send a repeating heartbeat while a button is held. Firmware stops the motors after about 300 to 500 ms of silence.
Medium Puts STBY on GPIO0 and GPIO2, and a driver input on GPIO15. These are boot-strapping pins. Their level at reset decides the boot mode, so attached hardware can block boot or make outputs glitch while the board starts. Check the pinout before assigning pins. Prefer non-strapping pins, or add pull resistors where the datasheet says so.
Medium Runs two motors in parallel on channel A of each TB6612FNG. Channel B stays unused. Troubleshooting says “driver gets hot”. Two stalled motors can exceed the rated 1.2 A continuous per channel. The hot driver is the symptom. One motor per channel. Measure real stall current and do the current budget on paper first.
Medium Returns motor ground through a mini breadboard rail with a single wire to the battery. Breadboard contacts are not made for amps. Voltage drops and noise reset the ESP or drop Wi-Fi. Star ground at the battery or a terminal block. Short, thick, soldered or screwed ground wires to each driver.
Medium Two 18650 cells straight to the circuit with no switch, fuse, or reverse-polarity protection. A short or a swapped wire turns into heat quickly. Nothing says how the cells are charged or whether they are protected cells. Add a power switch and an inline fuse or polyfuse. Use protected cells and a proper charger.
Low Publishes the Wi-Fi access point password (12345678) in the tutorial. Anyone nearby can join the car’s network and drive it. Unique password per car, set at build time.
Low handleCmd returns without a response if the argument is missing. Motion functions use blocking delay(). Uses SPIFFS and the ESP8266FS upload tool. Clients hang, the web server stalls during moves, and SPIFFS is deprecated in favour of LittleFS. The upload plugin may not work with newer Arduino IDE versions (check). Always send a response. Use millis() timing. Use LittleFS and verify the upload tooling for your IDE.
Low The ASCII power diagram contains leftover reasoning: “optional bridge … ? No – keep motor power off breadboard”. The model’s thinking leaked into the deliverable. Labels like “Buck #1 VIN (3.3V)” confuse input and output. Nobody re-read it. Redraw the schematic yourself. If the doc is not worth proofreading, it is not worth building from.
What it gets right: the 1 kΩ / 2 kΩ divider for the 5 V echo signal, setting the buck converters before connecting anything, keeping motor current off the breadboard, soft-start on the motors, and calling out polarised capacitors. Good content and bad content sit side by side, which is the skill: verify each claim on its own.
